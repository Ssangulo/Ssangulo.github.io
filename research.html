Seasonal turnover in the dung fungal community of willow ptarmigan

2022–present

In progress

Willow ptarmigan (Lagopus lagopus) switch diet sharply between a winter of woody browse and a summer of green forage. This project asks whether that dietary shift is written into the fungal community of their droppings. I sampled dung at Lifjellet, Lierne (Norway) in March and June across three years, sequenced fungal ITS2 alongside plant ITS2 from the same samples, and tested for seasonal compositional turnover, diversity change, and covariation between individual fungal taxa and the plants in the diet.

Every sample was amplified in uniquely indexed PCR replicates, which stay separate through the whole pipeline and enter the models as their own level, so technical variation is estimated rather than assumed away. The chain runs from demultiplexing and primer trimming through DADA2 denoising and LULU curation to UNITE-based taxonomy, then into community-level models. Robust-Aitchison PERMANOVA, Hill-number diversity, generalised linear latent variable models and a joint species distribution model. A separate strand documents the "dark" fungal taxa, sequences with no confident named match, against UNITE Species Hypotheses.

Parallel elevation filtering of root fungi on Andean sky islands

2019–2024

Preprint · in review

Above the Andean treeline, the páramo sits as a series of isolated mountaintops — “sky islands” separated by valleys that their alpine species cannot cross. That isolation makes them a natural experiment: if each mountaintop assembles its own idiosyncratic community, geography wins; if all four restructure the same way, environmental filtering does.

I used ITS2 metabarcoding to characterise the root-associated fungi of Gaultheria myrsinoides, a widespread pioneer ericaceous shrub, across forest–páramo transitions from 2,700 to 3,800 m on four mountaintops of the Colombian Andes. Both taxonomic and phylogenetic diversity fell sharply with elevation, and composition differed strongly among habitats.

The result was parallelism: despite strong isolation and site-specific baselines, the direction and magnitude of assembly were statistically consistent across all four mountaintops. Two dominant lineages drove it in opposite directions — Helotiales rose with elevation on the back of high-elevation specialists, while Sebacinales fell, which cuts against the prevailing view that Sebacinales dominates ericoid mycorrhizal communities across the tropical Andes.

The sampling design nests PCR replicates within root samples within plant individuals within locations, which also made it a useful testbed for how much of an apparent community pattern survives technical replication.

Permafrost decomposition on the Finnmark tundra — BSc thesis

2022–2023

My BSc thesis grew out of a field season at Iskoras, near Karasjok in northernmost Norway (~69.5°N), where thawing permafrost is releasing carbon that has been locked in frozen ground for millennia. How fast that carbon breaks down once it thaws is one of the larger open terms in projections of Arctic warming.

I measured decomposition using the Tea Bag Index — a deceptively simple standardised method in which commercial green and rooibos tea bags are buried, recovered after a fixed interval, and weighed. Because the two teas decompose at very different rates, the pair yields both a decomposition rate and a stabilisation factor, comparable against any other site in the world running the same protocol. Over a hundred bags went into the ground across the site's thaw gradient.

Part of that gradient sits under open-top chambers, passive warming enclosures that raise the temperature inside by a degree or two without power or attention. Burying the same tea bags inside and outside them turns the question from how fast carbon breaks down now into how fast it would break down in a warmer Arctic.

The work sat within the wider permafrost project led by Hanna Lee at the NTNU Department of Biology, where I first joined the site as a field assistant in the summer of 2022.

In the press Nancy Bazilchuk, “Tea bags — and more — on the tundra,” Norwegian SciTech News, 11 October 2023 — a visit to the Iskoras field site, including the tea-bag work.

Code & reproducibility

Every project here has a public repository holding the numbered scripts that produce its results, in order, from raw reads to final figures. Analyses are written in R and rendered with Quarto, so a supplementary appendix is rebuilt from the same source that produced the numbers in it, and cannot quietly drift out of step with them.

Looking for a specific script, dataset or intermediate object? Get in touch. I am glad to share.
